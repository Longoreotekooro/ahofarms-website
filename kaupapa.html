TE KAUPAPA

The values that govern everything we do

Kaupapa is not a marketing statement at Aho Farms. It is the operating framework that determines who we hire, how we treat the land, how we share the returns, and what kind of company we are building.

OUR FOUNDATION

"Tiakina te whenua, tiakina te iwi."

Guard the land, guard the people.

What our kaupapa means in practice

This kaupapa — guard the land, guard the people — is not a slogan. It is an obligation. Aho Farms was established on Māori freehold land because the whenua is inseparable from the enterprise. The land is not a resource to be extracted from. It is a taonga held in trust, to be returned to future generations in better condition than we found it.

Every decision we make is tested against this. How we grow — without synthetic pesticides, with attention to soil health and biodiversity. How we hire — with priority given to whānau and hapū. How we share returns — with reinvestment into the community that sustains us. This is not corporate social responsibility. This is how a Māori enterprise is supposed to work.

OUR OBLIGATIONS

Four obligations that shape every decision

1

Kaitiakitanga

Guardianship of the land

We grow on ancestral land. That privilege carries obligation. Our cultivation practices are designed to leave the soil, the water, and the ecosystem in better condition each season. We do not use synthetic pesticides or herbicides. We monitor soil health through the growing season and make management decisions based on long-term land health, not short-term yield maximisation.

Outdoor and greenhouse cultivation are not simply cost choices. They are a cultivation philosophy. Cannabis grown in natural conditions, under natural light cycles, produces a more complex terpene profile and develops a relationship with its environment. Where we grow indoors, that same care carries through in the precision and consistency it's built for.

Kaitiakitanga extends beyond the farm boundary. We source inputs locally where possible, minimise packaging waste, and are building towards full traceability of our environmental footprint.

2

Whānau

Family and community first

Our employment model starts with whānau. When we have a role to fill, we look within our immediate community first. This is not a quota. It is a recognition that sustainable enterprise is built on relationships of trust and mutual obligation, not transactional hiring.

We invest in our people beyond the wage. Whānau who join Aho Farms get access to training, pathway development, and the genuine prospect of advancement — including into governance roles as the company grows.

The whānau of the people who work here, and the hapū whose land this sits on, are stakeholders in this enterprise whether or not they are on the payroll. We take that seriously in how we make decisions and how we share the benefits of commercial success.

3

Mana

Integrity and reputation

Mana is earned through consistent, honest behaviour over time. In a regulated industry with a complicated public perception, maintaining our mana requires that we operate to a higher standard than the minimum required. We do not cut corners on compliance. We do not make claims we cannot verify. We do not take shortcuts on quality.

This applies to how we communicate. The medicinal cannabis industry has attracted operators who overstate their products, make therapeutic claims they are not permitted to make, and market to consumers in ways that breach advertising rules. We have chosen a different path: conservative, compliant, and honest.

Our prescribers, pharmacy partners, and investors trust us because we behave consistently. That trust is our most important commercial asset and we protect it accordingly.

4

Oranga

Wellbeing and health

Medicinal cannabis is a health product. The patients who ultimately receive our product are people in pain, people with compromised sleep, people managing anxiety or serious illness. Their wellbeing is the end point of everything we do.

We are growers and suppliers, not clinicians. We do not treat patients and we do not make therapeutic claims. But we recognise that the quality and consistency of the product we grow has a direct bearing on patient outcomes. That recognition shapes how seriously we take every step from seed to laboratory test.

Oranga also applies to our own people. A company that depletes its workforce is not building sustainable enterprise. We invest in the health, the development, and the wellbeing of our team as a core obligation, not an afterthought.

OUR FOUNDATION

The Waaka whānau partnership

The land in Hawke's Bay on which Aho Farms operates is Māori freehold land held by the Waaka whānau. Our partnership with the Waaka whānau is the foundation of this enterprise — not a lease arrangement or a commercial convenience, but a genuine shared commitment to the productive and responsible development of their whenua.

Māori freehold land carries obligations that general land title does not. It cannot simply be sold. It must be used for the benefit of the owners and their descendants. Aho Farms is one expression of what that can look like: a licensed, compliant, commercial enterprise that generates income from the land, employs the people whose land it is, and returns value to the community.

This is the model we are building. It is not finished. It is not perfect. But it is genuine, and it is grounded in the land and the people in a way that cannot be replicated by operators without this foundation.
