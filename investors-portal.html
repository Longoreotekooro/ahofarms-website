WHY AHO FARMS

Why Aho Farms

Land advantage. Māori freehold land in Hawke's Bay means no lease risk, structural cost advantage, and access to whenua-based enterprise support mechanisms unavailable to other operators. The land cannot be acquired by competitors, and the cost base it enables is structurally lower than leased or purchased freehold alternatives.

Licence held. Cultivation licence active under the NZ Misuse of Drugs Act. Export licence held. These are material barriers to entry — obtaining new licences from the Ministry of Health takes 12–18+ months, involves significant compliance infrastructure, and is not guaranteed. Aho holds both. Competitors starting today are 18 months behind.

Team with California depth. Longo Reo Te Kooro and Michael Maclean bring genuine cultivation and operations expertise from California's mature recreational market — a jurisdiction with more regulatory maturity, more operational scale, and more product sophistication than anywhere in New Zealand. This experience base is rare and not easily acquired.

Market position. NZ and AU markets are in early growth phase. Aho is ahead of the scaling curve — product in market, prescriber relationships being built, and export infrastructure active. First-mover positioning in prescriber adoption creates durable distribution advantages as the market grows.

Cultural differentiation. Māori ownership is not a marketing angle — it's a structural advantage for iwi investment, government procurement, Māori economic development funding, and media attention. These create brand value, access to capital channels, and distribution preferences that no competitor — however well-funded — can simply purchase.

MARKET

The Market

New Zealand

42 licence holders competing in a domestic market that is still early in prescriber adoption. Prescriptions have grown consistently since scheme launch in 2020. A gap exists between patient demand and compliant local supply — the import dependency creates both a short-term opportunity and a structural case for domestic scale.

Australia

Australia is the primary export target and the largest medicinal cannabis market in the Southern Hemisphere. Over 1 million active patients, 80,000+ prescriptions per month, and accelerating prescriber adoption — yet 78%+ of Australian doctors have still not yet prescribed medicinal cannabis. The market is early, not mature.

TGA-licensed NZ exporters are well positioned. Australia's domestic cultivation base has not scaled to meet demand — import dependency creates sustained supply opportunity for export-licensed NZ producers with documented quality systems.

Global Context

The United Kingdom, Germany, Israel, and EU member states represent medium-term export diversification opportunities. Post-German legalisation, EU demand is escalating rapidly. NZ's regulatory credibility and IANZ testing infrastructure create a foundation for access to premium-paying markets where product provenance and documentation are requirements, not differentiators.

ABOUT

About Aho Farms

Aho Farms Limited is a Māori-owned medicinal cannabis enterprise based in Hawke's Bay, New Zealand. Founded by Longo Reo Te Kooro in partnership with the Waaka whānau, the company holds cultivation and export licences under the New Zealand Medicinal Cannabis Scheme and currently has two products in the NZ market with export supply underway to Australia.

Company Timeline

2020

Company founded. Partnership formed with Waaka whānau on Māori freehold land, Hawke's Bay.

2021–22

Licence applications lodged with Ministry of Health. Site preparation and compliance infrastructure development begins.

2023

Cultivation licence granted under the NZ Medicinal Cannabis Scheme. Michael Maclean joins as Cultivation Director.

2024

First harvest completed. Laboratory testing partnership established with Hill Labs, Hamilton (IANZ accredited). Board of Directors formalised.

2025

First supply to NZ prescribers. RĀ and SOURCE product lines launched. Prescriber portal established.

2026

Export licence active. Second successful harvest season. Distribution partnerships under development.

Current Status

Two products in market — RĀ and SOURCE — through pharmacy and prescriber channel. Prescriber portal live and active. Pharmacy relationships being built across NZ. Export programme underway. Second harvest season successfully completed 2026.

Pipeline

Cultivar diversification is planned for the 2026–27 season. CBD-dominant and balanced THC:CBD products are under evaluation to address a wider range of prescriber preferences and patient conditions. Volume expansion is directly tied to prescriber adoption growth and export contract development.

Competitive Positioning

Cost structure. Our sun-grown outdoor tier delivers a materially lower cost-per-gram than our indoor or greenhouse environments — a structural cost advantage built into how we grow, not a temporary pricing strategy.

Quality documentation. IANZ-accredited testing via Hill Labs means every batch carries documented CoA (Certificate of Analysis) data that meets export-market requirements. Quality is not asserted — it is documented.

Brand advantages. Māori ownership creates brand positioning — particularly in Māori health procurement, iwi-linked distribution channels, and premium export markets where provenance and story carry commercial value.

WHENUA & WHAKAPAPA

The Whenua Foundation

Aho Farms is different from every other medicinal cannabis company in New Zealand in one irreducible way: the land in Hawke's Bay is Māori freehold land, and the enterprise is built on it, with the people of that land, under their governance. This is not a corporate social responsibility programme. It is the commercial and cultural structure of the business.

Waaka Whānau Partnership

The partnership between the founding team and the Waaka whānau is the foundation of the enterprise. The whenua provides land tenure security unavailable to non-Māori operators. The relationship provides cultural legitimacy, community employment pathways, and governance participation that shapes how decisions are made and how returns flow.

"Tiakina te whenua, tiakina te iwi." — Founding kaupapa of Aho Farms

Tiakina te Whenua

Māori values shape governance practice at Aho Farms. Kaitiakitanga — guardianship of the land — informs cultivation methods. Manaakitanga informs employment practice. Whanaungatanga informs how the business relates to community stakeholders, prescribers, patients, and investors. These are not aspirational statements. They are operational commitments reflected in governance documentation and board composition.

Employment Commitment

Priority Māori employment is embedded in the enterprise model. As the business scales, employment pathways to governance — from cultivation through to board advisory — are a structural commitment. This creates a talent pipeline and community alignment that strengthens both operational capability and social licence.

NZTE and Callaghan Innovation

Aho Farms is eligible for Māori export and enterprise development support through New Zealand Trade and Enterprise and Callaghan Innovation. These mechanisms — including Māori-specific funding streams, export market development grants, and R&D co-funding — represent capital-efficient pathways to scale that are unavailable to non-Māori enterprises.

Why This Matters to Investors

Māori economic development is a stated priority of successive New Zealand governments and is embedded in Treaty partnership obligations. Businesses with genuine Māori foundations have access to funding channels, procurement preferences, media attention, and institutional relationships that create durable competitive advantages. This is a commercial reality — not a values statement. For investors, the Māori dimension of Aho Farms is not a risk to be assessed; it is a structural asset to be understood.
