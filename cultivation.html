Kāinga

Controlled cultivation and processing

Our 396m² indoor facility supports propagation, mother plant maintenance, drying and processing.

Kāinga gives us the control required to protect consistency, genetics and quality across every cultivation cycle.

Māra

Cultivation connected to the whenua

Our Māra site includes approximately one acre of outdoor cultivation and 14 greenhouses.

It allows us to work across natural and protected environments while providing capacity to expand production as demand grows.

Control at Kāinga. Expression at Māra.

GENETICS SELECTED FOR OUR ENVIRONMENT
89 12

89 mother plants. 12 selected expressions.

Our cultivation programme began with an extensive phenotype hunt designed to identify genetics that perform within the environments in which we actually grow.

From 89 mother plants, 12 were selected based on their suitability to our cultivation conditions and the characteristics they expressed within them.

Rather than forcing every plant into the same system, we select genetics that work with the environment around them.

HAWKE’S BAY TERROIR

The Influence of Place

Hawke’s Bay has long been recognised for what its land can produce.

Its soils, sunlight, seasonal warmth and Pacific coastal influence create distinct growing environments across the region.

For Aho, those conditions are not simply a backdrop to cultivation.

They are part of it.

SoilSunlightAirTemperatureWaterGenetics

Together, these elements influence how a plant grows and expresses itself.

This is our interpretation of terroir.

Hawke’s Bay is more than where we grow. It is part of the cultivar.
THE 39TH PARALLEL

A Parallel Across the Pacific

Hawke’s Bay sits at approximately the 39th parallel south.

Across the equator, Northern California’s renowned cannabis-growing regions sit around the corresponding northern latitudes.

The environments are not identical. But the geographical parallel is compelling.

Both are Pacific-influenced regions shaped by seasonal light, local soils, climate and generations of agricultural knowledge.

For us, the comparison raises a simple question:

What can exceptional cannabis expression look like in Hawke’s Bay?
