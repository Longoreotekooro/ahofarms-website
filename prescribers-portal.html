Clinical Evidence Summaries

The following summaries are provided to support clinical decision-making. They are not exhaustive and do not constitute prescribing recommendations.

Chronic Pain

Multiple RCTs demonstrate modest but consistent analgesic effects of THC-containing cannabis medicines in chronic non-cancer pain. Evidence is strongest for neuropathic pain subtypes. A 2021 Cochrane review found moderate-quality evidence for reduction in pain scores.

Aviram & Samuelly-Leichtag (2017); Aviram (2021 Cochrane)

Chronic pain is the most common approved condition in NZ. Over 60% of TGA-approved indications in AU are for chronic pain.

Sleep Disorders

Short-term evidence supports THC-containing preparations for sleep onset and duration in patients with comorbid conditions (pain, anxiety, PTSD). Long-term data limited. A 2023 systematic review found cannabinoids improved subjective sleep quality.

Bachhuber et al (2019); Suraev et al (2020)

Sleep is the second most common indication in NZ. Often comorbid with pain and anxiety.

Anxiety

Preclinical evidence is strong; RCT evidence in humans is limited but emerging. CBD-dominant preparations show promise for anxiety disorders. High-THC products may exacerbate anxiety in some patients — patient selection is critical.

Blessing et al (2015); Kayser et al (2023)

Mental health is the third most common NZ indication. Particular attention to patient history of psychosis or THC sensitivity.

For a comprehensive evidence database, see the New Zealand Medical Cannabis Council (NZMCC) clinical resources and the RACGP Medicinal Cannabis Clinical Guidance. Both are updated periodically as new evidence emerges.

Dosing Guidance

Clinical Disclaimer: The following guidance is indicative only and does not replace clinical judgement. Dosing should be individualised based on patient history, previous cannabis exposure, indication, and concurrent medications.

Starting Dose Recommendations

  • Cannabis-naive patients: Start low, go slow. Begin with CBD-dominant or balanced product where possible. If a THC product is clinically indicated, start at 1–2.5 mg THC per dose.
  • Previous cannabis users: May tolerate higher starting doses. Still recommend conservative start with gradual titration.
  • Elderly patients: Start at lowest dose. Higher sensitivity to psychoactive effects. Monitor closely and titrate slowly.

Titration Protocol

  • Increase dose no more than every 3–7 days
  • Titrate to effect or to tolerance limit
  • Document dose changes and patient response at each step
  • Regular review at 4 weeks and 12 weeks recommended

Dried Flower — Inhalation Guidance

  • Inhalation (vaporisation) has faster onset (5–15 minutes) and shorter duration (2–3 hours) than oil preparations
  • Combustion (smoking) is not recommended — advise patients to use a calibrated dry-herb vaporiser
  • Effects are variable between patients — advise patients to use in a safe environment and at home initially
  • Advise against driving or operating machinery until individual response is established

Drug Interactions to Consider

  • CNS depressants: Additive sedation with benzodiazepines, opioids, and alcohol — monitor carefully
  • Anticoagulants: Cannabis may affect warfarin metabolism — monitor INR more frequently on initiation
  • Antiepileptics: Possible pharmacokinetic interactions — seek specialist advice before co-prescribing
  • CYP450 interactions: CBD is a CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 inhibitor at higher doses — review concurrent medications

How to Prescribe Medicinal Cannabis in New Zealand

A step-by-step guide to initiating and managing medicinal cannabis prescribing under the New Zealand regulatory framework.

  1. 1

    Patient Assessment

    Confirm the patient has a condition for which medicinal cannabis may be appropriate. Document diagnosis, previous treatments trialled, and your clinical rationale. Consider contraindications including personal or family history of psychosis, current substance use disorder, and pregnancy or breastfeeding.

  2. 2

    Product Selection

    Select an appropriate product based on indication, required onset and duration, patient preference, and cost. Contact Aho Farms prescriber support for product information and current availability. Consider THC:CBD ratio, delivery route, and whether the patient has prior cannabis exposure.

  3. 3

    Regulatory Requirements

    Medicinal cannabis products are prescription medicines in NZ — standard prescribing rules apply. Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) products require specific prescribing authority. Confirm your prescribing rights and whether your DHB or organisation has any additional internal requirements.

  4. 4

    Dispensing

    The patient takes their prescription to a pharmacy. Pharmacies must have the product in stock or order it. Aho Farms products are available through our pharmacy network — contact us for current stockist details. Dispensing fees and consultation fees are not subsidised by PHARMAC.

  5. 5

    Patient Monitoring

    Review at 4 weeks to assess efficacy, side effects, and dose appropriateness. Document outcomes in the patient record. Ongoing 3-monthly reviews are recommended for stable patients. Reassess the benefit/risk balance at each review.

  6. 6

    Ongoing Support

    Aho Farms prescriber support is available for product questions, CoA requests, and clinical queries. Contact: prescribers@ahofarms.co.nz. Our team can arrange calls for complex queries.

NZ Prescribing Guidelines: Refer to Medsafe guidance on medicinal cannabis prescribing and the Ministry of Health Medicinal Cannabis Scheme resources at health.govt.nz. The NZMCC also publishes regularly updated prescriber resources.
